## From triangle to circle

In a right triangle an angle can’t go past 90°. So what could sin 150° or cos 300° even mean?

The trick: put the triangle in **standard position**, with one corner at the centre of a circle and one side on the x axis. Make the hypotenuse (the radius) equal to **1**. Now:

- <span class="c-cos">cos θ</span> = x / 1 = the point’s **x coordinate**;
- <span class="c-sin">sin θ</span> = y / 1 = the point’s **y coordinate**;
- <span class="c-tan">tan θ</span> = y / x = the **slope** of the radius.

Now the radius can spin as far as you like. The packet calls this *“Spin around”*: the triangle spins and the circle stays put.

## The signs in each quadrant

As the point spins, x and y change sign:

| quadrant | angles | positive |
|---|---|---|
| I | 0°–90° | all |
| II | 90°–180° | only sin |
| III | 180°–270° | only tan (− divided by − is +) |
| IV | 270°–360° | only cos |

## The reference angle

150° looks exactly like 30°, just mirrored into quadrant II. So sin 150° = sin 30° = ½, and the cosine is the same size but negative. The packet calls this *“Cycle down”*: bring any angle back to one between 0° and 90°, then add the quadrant’s sign.

## The special angles

These are worth knowing by heart:

| θ | 0° | 30° | 45° | 60° | 90° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sin θ | 0 | 1/2 | √2/2 | √3/2 | 1 |
| cos θ | 1 | √3/2 | √2/2 | 1/2 | 0 |

Notice anything? The cos row is the sin row read backwards.

## From circle to wave

To the right of the circle, the point’s height is drawn against the angle. The result is a wave that repeats every 360°: **the graph of sine**. The blue line, cosine, is the same wave slid 90° to the left. In the next lab you’ll shape these waves yourself.

> Radians: a full turn is 360°, which is 2π radians. So 180° = π, 90° = π/2 and 60° = π/3.
